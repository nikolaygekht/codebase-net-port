3. WHY 1.43x AND 2.45x MOVE A SEEK BY HALF A PER CENT

  A 4-level seek is about three interior searches and one leaf scan:

    saved = 3 x (15.156 - 6.175) + (211.477 - 147.981) = ~90 ns

  against a 9 238 ns seek. 1%. The audit measured that ~60% of a file-backed
  seek is read syscalls (4.286 reads at 1.18-1.21 us each), and the whole
  descent is a small slice of the remainder, so removing work from it cannot
  show up end to end while the syscalls are still there.

  This is a CORRECTION TO THE FRAMING, not to the numbers. DESIGN.md's
  finding table reads "P2 ... 1.7-1.9x of the descent", which invites reading
  it as 1.7-1.9x of a seek. It is 1.7-1.9x of the comparison work, and the
  comparison work is ~1% of a file-backed seek and ~13% of a cached one
  (~90 ns of the audit's 680 ns cached seek). P2 was worth doing -- it is
  free, it is measured, and it removes 29-43% of the allocation on the hot
  path -- but it is not where the time is. P1, the block cache, is.

  T_NAME   the library, compare in place  531.7 ns      2.60 KB    1.25x

WHY THE LIBRARY IS 1.15-1.32x OFF THE HAND-INLINED LOOP

  Identical allocation, so it is not doing more work -- it is paying per-entry
  call overhead the hand-written loop does not have. Build() is a call per
  compared entry, plus its two corruption checks (a stored length below zero,
  key text running into the entry array) which the harness omitted and the
  library must not. 71 ns over 21 entries and 166 ns over 50 is ~3.3 ns an
  entry, and the numeric tag pays it 50 times per seek, which is why T_AMT
  suffers more.

  Inlining LeafBlock.Seek's scan and hoisting the geometry into a field DID
  NOT close it: the gap was 1.14x / 1.34x before that change and 1.15x / 1.32x
  after. THAT CHANGE WAS THEREFORE REVERTED -- it bought nothing measurable
  and made Seek drive builtIndex directly, which is more coupling for no
  return. "No optimization without a measurement" cuts both ways, and this is
  the second time this step has had to apply it to its own work.

  Closing the rest means duplicating Build inside Seek, or making it small
  enough to inline by moving its throws out of line. NOT DONE, and
  deliberately -- see the sizing below.

WHAT IT IS WORTH, AND WHY WE STOP HERE

  against a file-backed seek (9 189 ns)        133 ns    1.4%
  against a RAM-backed descent (531.7 ns)      133 ns    20%
  the residual, against a RAM-backed descent    71-166 ns   13-24%

  P2 buys nothing measurable while a seek is five read syscalls, and about a
  fifth of it once those are gone. The residual is worth the same order again
  -- but only in the same, cached, world. So it is measured, written down, and
  left, to be revisited after the cache exists and can be measured where it
  matters. "No optimization without a measurement" cuts both ways.

AND THE ELEPHANT, CORROBORATED WITHOUT BORROWING THE AUDIT'S NUMBERS

  the same descent, through a file        9 189 ns
  the same descent, from RAM               ~530 ns      ~17x

  That is today's data, on today's build, and it is what P1 -- the block cache
  -- converts one into the other. The 11.6x and 23.4x in SUMMARY.md are the
  same fact measured a different way. P2 was never claimed to be that, and it
  is not that.

8. THE DENOMINATOR NOBODY HAD MEASURED: HOW MUCH OF A DESCENT IS ALLOCATION

  The question that produced this section: compare-in-place removed ~1.2 KB
  per seek and bought 20% of a RAM-backed descent, but what SHARE of the
  descent is allocation at all? Nobody knew, because 2.6 KB per seek was
  still being allocated after the change.

  Fourth arm, identical to the library's in-place descent except that the
  block is read into ONE REUSED byte[512] instead of a fresh one per read:

  tag      arm                                    time      allocated
  T_NAME   key copied per comparison            690.3 ns      3 860 B
  T_NAME   the library, compare in place        553.2 ns      2 664 B
  T_NAME   audit's hand-inlined loop            477.1 ns      2 664 B
  T_NAME   in place + block buffer reused       339.7 ns        296 B

  ALLOCATION IS ~40% OF A CACHED DESCENT -- 213 of 553 ns, which is MORE than
  the whole compare-in-place win (137 ns). Allocation per seek falls 9x, and
  the descent is 1.63x faster than the library's current path and 1.40x
  faster than the hand-inlined loop we could not reach.

  T_AMT was multimodal in this run (StdDev 61 ns, and its in-place arm moved
  683 -> 830 ns between runs). Not leaned on. T_NAME is the clean signal, and
  the allocation counts are exact on both.

  Note the arms are not additive with section 6's: removing the per-comparison
  copies made the per-read buffer a LARGER share of what remains, which is
  why it has to be measured after them and not predicted from them.

WHY THIS IS NOT A FOOTNOTE

  DESIGN.md puts the byte[512] outside the cache's scope, in an Open question
  that CONFLATES TWO DIFFERENT JOBS:

    hard: hand out the POOL's array. Needs pinning or refcounting, because a
          LeafBlock keeps its array for its whole life and a TagCursor keeps
          blocks across calls. Rightly deferred.
    easy: let the CALLER reuse its own buffer instead of allocating per read.
          Needs only what a cursor already guarantees -- about 3 of the 5
          reads per seek are branch blocks, read then searched then descended
          then discarded. Two buffers per cursor (one for the descent path,
          one for the leaf it is parked on) replace 5 allocations per seek.

  Deferring the hard one deferred the easy one with it. The easy one is worth
  ~40% of a cached descent, which is the same order as everything else in this
  step put together.

AND A DECISION THE PORT HAS NEVER MADE

  There is NO documented threading contract: no locks, no [ThreadStatic], no
  statement in any document. That is fine while every buffer is per-call, and
  it stops being fine the moment anything is reused -- including the cache
  itself, because DESIGN.md decision 4 makes the pool ENGINE-WIDE, and an
  engine-wide pool touched from two threads is a data race with or without
  buffer reuse.

  Worth being precise about the risk, because it is easy to name the wrong
  one: the danger of reusing a block buffer is NOT primarily threading, it is
  OBJECT LIFETIME WITHIN ONE THREAD. LeafBlock rebuilds keys out of the array
  it was handed, so overwriting that array under a live LeafBlock corrupts
  reads on the same thread. A per-thread buffer does not fix that; a lifetime
  rule does, and per-thread only closes the extra hole that sharing one buffer
  between threads would open.

  So the cache step owes two things it was not going to write down:
    - what the threading contract IS (proposal: a Table and its cursors are
      single-threaded, an engine may be shared only for opening tables), and
    - caller-side buffer reuse, sized by the numbers above.
